## Abstract

This dissertation examines the antidemocratic thought emergent in the intersection of Silicon Valley technolibertarianism and alt-right politics in the early 21st century. Tracing the proliferation of "red pill" epistemology from its origins in *The Matrix* to its adoption by online misogynist communities and tech entrepreneurs, it argues that contemporary reactionary movements represent a neoliberal response to the crisis of symbolic efficiency, where traditional sources of meaning and authority have deteriorated under datafied capitalism.

Through case studies of key figures including Jordan Peterson, Peter Thiel, Curtis Yarvin, and their intellectual appropriation of thinkers like René Girard and Austrian school economists, it demonstrates how computational logics—particularly axiomatization, algorithmic thinking, and "first principles" methodology—have become tools for encoding white supremacist and patriarchal worldviews as objective truths.

Keywords: technofascism, critical theory, critical algorithm studies, media studies, alt-right, cyberlibertarianism.
